## Claim
A working paper ("Does Rank Still Matter? Position Bias When AI Agents Shop on Our Behalf," arXiv:2608.22697) randomized the order of 100 hotel listings across 5,000 AI-agent shopping sessions across 4 LLMs. Position still predicts which listings get inspected, but weakly and non-monotonically — the middle of a results page had the lowest inspection probability, not the bottom — and the effect pattern varies by model in ways unrelated to provider or capability tier. All models nonetheless converged on the same undominated listing.

## Why it matters
Direct empirical evidence that LLM-agent decisions over ranked/ordered inputs can be order-sensitive independent of content — a reproducibility risk for any Hermes/OpenClaw agent that ranks or triages ordered lists (catalyst queues, filing review order, coinvest candidates). A companion study cited in the paper found that prompting an agent to "ignore position" did not fix the effect.

## Underlying source
https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22697 (also cross-referenced via the-decoder.com and SSRN)

## Verification
Not yet independently reproduced against Wake Robin's own agents. Domain is consumer shopping (hotel listings), not finance — extrapolation to filing/catalyst ranking is an inference, not a demonstrated result.

## Potential workflow impact
Flags an evaluation gap in any Hermes agent that ranks/selects among ordered inputs (selector/ranker, catalyst triage, coinvest candidate lists) — order alone could shift output even when content is fixed.

## Smallest shadow test
Re-run one existing Hermes ranking/triage task with input order shuffled 20-30 times, holding content fixed, and check whether the output ranking is stable. Reuses the existing test harness — no new infrastructure needed.

## Final disposition
Hold for shadow-research backlog review. Do not re-elevate without material change (independent replication, a finance-domain version of the study, or a Hermes-internal test result) until after 2026-09-11.
