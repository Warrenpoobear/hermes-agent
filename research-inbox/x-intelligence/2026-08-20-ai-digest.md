## Claim
Sadhu et al. show that current LLM-based compliance/guard-model detectors exhibit "rule blindness": using a crossed-rule benchmark (two rules x two scenarios, where neither rule nor scenario alone predicts the verdict), deleting, permuting, or substituting the governing rule leaves many detectors' verdicts unchanged — evidence they classify on scenario surface features, not the stated rule.

## Why it matters
Directly relevant to any compliance/guard-model layer Darren might rely on inside Reliability Gate v1.0 (PR #19) or biotech-screener write-path governance. A guard model that doesn't actually read the rule gives false assurance exactly where PR #19 needs real assurance — this is a falsifiable failure mode to test for before trusting any guardrail component (including the already-tracked Mistral Shieldstral, surfaced 2026-08-09).

## Underlying source
arXiv:2608.16852, "What Do Compliance Detectors Read? An Audit of Activation Probes and Guard Models," submitted 2026-08-17. Secondary coverage: Tech Times (Aug 19, 2026), timed to the EU AI Act's Aug 2, 2026 full-applicability date.

## Verification
Single arXiv paper, not yet independently replicated; no response yet from guard-model vendors on this specific failure mode. Classification: primary evidence, confidence medium pending replication.

## Potential workflow impact
Before relying on any guard-model/compliance-detector component in Reliability Gate v1.0, test it against a crossed-rule benchmark analogous to Sadhu et al.'s design to confirm it is not scenario-pattern-matching.

## Smallest shadow test
Build 5-10 hand-crafted rule/scenario pairs, swap the governing rule while holding the scenario fixed, and check whether the Reliability Gate's candidate guard model's verdict changes. If it doesn't, that is direct evidence of rule blindness in our own stack.
