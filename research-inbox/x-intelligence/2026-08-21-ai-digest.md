## Claim
Up to four parallel OpenAI Codex agents, kicked off from a five-sentence prompt, migrated Asana's frontend test suite off the unmaintained Enzyme library onto React Testing Library. Asana's internal staffing estimate for the manual project was five years and roughly $6-8.5M; the agent-run migration merged in about 1.5 weeks of actual engineering effort spread across two calendar weeks, at roughly $12,000-$17,000 in model and infrastructure cost. Engineers limited themselves to twice-daily check-ins and reviewed every change.

This is a first-party (Asana's own engineering blog, cross-confirmed by OpenAI's case study and independent outlets) example of parallel coding-agent orchestration collapsing a large, mechanical, multi-year tech-debt project into weeks. ## Claim
"AQuA: Recursively Self-Improving Quantitative Trading Research Agents" (arXiv:2608.12841, submitted ~Aug 13, 2026) describes two independent language-model-driven research systems -- a manager-mediated multi-agent factor-discovery pipeline and a config-driven time-series model-development loop -- that each recursively improve by retaining validated evidence from earlier experiments inside sealed sandboxes (data splits, labels, and evaluator held fixed; the model can act only through constrained factor expressions or config diffs). The factor system reaches a combined information coefficient of ~0.190 on a crypto universe; the model system reaches a per-stock IC of +0.0843 on US equities, converted into a threshold long/short strategy with a held-out Sharpe up to +2.50, positive every year from 2021 to 2025.

## Why it matters
This is a generalizable design pattern -- not a vendor pitch -- for automating the propose-test-retain-repropose research loop that underlies Darren's screener factor/backtest work, with an explicit anti-overfitting guard (sealed sandbox, fixed evaluator, constrained action space) rather than free-form model retraining. Directly relevant to the backtest-framework and regime-detection skills' PIT-safety and look-ahead-prevention concerns.
